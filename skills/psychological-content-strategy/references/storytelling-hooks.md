# Storytelling hooks by goal

Use this companion to the [hook library](hook-library.md) when a real event, decision, client interaction, or process gives the viewer a reason to stay. The ten story lenses adapt the supplied Chase Dimond gallery. Each lens guards a different promise. The niche examples are original fictional teaching scenarios, with assets and events stipulated for each row.

For examples from hook to payoff, use the [timed Shorts scripts with GIFs](shorts-story-examples.md).

## Choose the story the material supports

| Lens | Required material and how to use it |
|---|---|
| Transformation | Comparable earlier and later states, plus the actual change that connects them. An outcome alone calls for a demonstration or a scoped result, depending on the evidence |
| Mistake | A specific misstep or missed cue, its observed consequence, and a correction the viewer can use |
| Turning point | A distinct event, observation, or conversation that changed a decision. Identify what the speaker changed as a result |
| Behind the scenes | A real step the viewer rarely gets to see. Show the decision, check, or work behind the finished artifact |
| Contrarian | A fair account of common advice and an evidenced condition where another approach fits better |
| Relatable | A recognizable situation with a detail the audience can place. Follow recognition with useful help or an honest reflection |
| Client or customer | A real question, project, or result with authority to share it. Name the evidence and scope. Use a sanitized example when the story itself can be told but identifying assets cannot be shown |
| Curiosity | A visible clue or specific question. Provide a useful clue early and resolve the actual question |
| Lesson | A real experience and the reusable check, rule, or decision it taught. Show where the viewer can apply it |
| Decision | Real alternatives and the criterion that decided the choice. Surface the tradeoff, including what the selected option gives up |

For a reenactment, identify it as such wherever the viewer could reasonably mistake it for archival proof. With stills, use an honest before/after, a labeled comparison, or a detail crop when appropriate. A single photo cannot substantiate a process or a change over time by itself. With text-only footage, start with the exact short overlay and ensure the first useful beat reads on mute. Follow the user's production scope.

## Relevant reach and recognition

| Niche and lens | Voice and available material | Opening words | First frame → next useful beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighborhood café · relatable | Dry and neighborly. Real shots of a fogged window and the reading corner | “Rain again. The window seat has plans for you.” | Open on the window droplets → cut to the reading corner and let the shot establish the setting |
| Thrift styling · curiosity | Warm and observant. Two stills of one jacket with and without a rolled cuff | “Same jacket. What changed at the sleeve?” | Place the stills side by side with matched crops → highlight the cuff and show where the sleeve now ends |
| Running club · relatable | Easygoing and welcoming. Silent route clip and the meeting landmark | “First run with us? Look for the blue gate.” | Use the gate visible in the route clip and the line as text → freeze on the meeting point and show where the group starts |
| Independent bookstore · decision | Literary and plainspoken. Two jackets and the bookseller's actual selection note | “For book club, we picked the one with the harder ending to agree on.” | Show both books from the selection → read the short selection note and give a spoiler-free discussion question |
| Museum · behind the scenes | Restrained and precise. Case-installation clip showing an actual label check and adjustment | “Here’s how we check whether a label is readable through the glass.” | Show the label from the visitor's side of the glass → show the change in position made for this display |

## Education and saves

| Niche and lens | Voice and available material | Opening words | First frame → next useful beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home baking · mistake | Kind and concrete. The baker's two test cookies, one with a scorched base, and the tray-position note | “I checked the tops. I missed what was happening underneath.” | Turn over the two cookies → show the recorded tray positions and give one check for the next batch |
| Language teaching · lesson | Patient and specific. Two original caption cards and the teacher's own pronunciation audio | “When these two words sound alike, listen for where the stress lands.” | Show the two words in large text → play the supplied pair and mark the stressed syllables |
| Home organization · contrarian | Calm and candid. Pantry stills showing an outlet and the bins that cover it | “In this pantry, matching bins would cover the outlet.” | Open on the outlet in the actual space → show the smaller bin arrangement and the gap it leaves |
| Bike repair · curiosity | Dry and technical. Close-up of a moving wheel with a visibly rubbing fender, plus clear source audio | “Hear that scrape? Look at the gap above the tire.” | Use the scrape and label the fender gap for mute → freeze on the visible contact point |
| Photography education · transformation | Observational. The photographer's two photos of the same cup and a setup shot showing the moved lamp | “Moving the lamp changed where the shadow fell on this cup.” | Match the two frames → show the old and new lamp positions and invite the viewer to try it with one object |

|---|---|---|---|

## Qualified inquiries, visits, and sales

| Niche and lens | Voice and available material | Opening words | First frame → next useful beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tailor · decision | Practical and reassuring. Same jacket pinned at the hem and cuff, with an active appointment route | “Deciding what to alter on a jacket? Check the sleeve with your arm bent.” | Show the model's cuff with the elbow bent → compare against the straight-arm frame, then invite a fitting |
| Hotel · decision | Quiet and precise. Real room clips and verified layouts for two room types | “Bringing two suitcases? Here’s the floor space beside each bed.” | Show one room from the doorway → compare matching angles of the two rooms before showing room options |
| B2B support tool · client or customer | Matter-of-fact. Approved anonymized question, sandbox recording, and a live demo page | “A support lead asked where an unassigned ticket goes. Here’s the queue it lands in.” | Open on an example ticket in the unassigned queue → replay the assignment state, then direct interested teams to the demo |
| Print shop · lesson | Friendly and exact. Actual proof sheet showing where a border crops and a documented revised proof | “Ordering postcards with a border? Check this edge on the proof.” | Hold on the real crop difference → mark the edge on the revised proof before inviting a proof request |
| Maker workshop · behind the scenes | Welcoming and practical. Real bench and tools, with verified session and booking details | “Here’s the bench you’ll use at your first printmaking class.” | Open on the set-up bench → point out what the class provides, then give the actual booking route |

Adapt the example to the real objective. A strong sales or booking opening gives a viewer enough information to judge fit. If a destination or offer detail is missing, finish on the useful answer and name what needs checking only if the user requested a production or conversion plan.

## Same material, different brand voice

Take a print-shop detail photo and a documented revised proof. For a precise specialist, write “Check the outside border on the trimmed proof” and lead with the crop comparison. For a friendly teacher, write “Can you see where the border gets cut?” and reveal the marked edge after a short hold. For a spare editorial brand with silent footage, use “Border at the cut edge” on the crop and let the revised proof carry the explanation. Each version expresses the same fact and can use the same body. The brand changes the delivery, and the available media determines the shot and text.
