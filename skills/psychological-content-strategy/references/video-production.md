# Short-form structure, editing, and production

Use this reference for a shootable script, storyboard, edit plan, or script-level audit. Timing examples are starting shapes, not platform requirements.

For an educational tutorial or longer YouTube script, use [the five-phase scriptwriting guide](educational-scriptwriting.md) to establish the learning contract, audience depth, research gap, transformation, and causal flow. Then use this reference for feasible beats and production. Keep complexity when it fulfills the learning promise; improve explanation and orientation before shortening it away.

## Three retention layers

| Layer | Viewer reason to stay | Production question |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic | Wants the answer, process, result, or next story beat | Does each beat move the promise forward? |
| Sensory | Can easily follow what changes | Do framing, sound, captions, and cuts clarify the idea? |
| Emotional | Feels recognition, curiosity, surprise, concern, humor, or aspiration | Does the feeling fit the story and audience? |

Repair meaning before adding stimulation. An uninteresting or confusing premise rarely becomes useful through more zooms. Give complex evidence enough time to be understood.

## Structural starting points

### Approximately 15 seconds: utility or demonstration

| Time | Job |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Show the result or problem; make the hook legible |
| 2–5 | Establish the relevant situation and first proof |
| 5–10 | Demonstrate one change or two compact steps |
| 10–13 | Deliver the rule, result, or complete answer |
| 13–15 | Give one natural next step, if useful |

Do not squeeze three dense explanations into this shape. Reduce the promise or extend the runtime when necessary.

### Approximately 30 seconds: tutorial or diagnosis

| Time | Job |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Promise and immediate visual evidence |
| 3–6 | One sentence of relevant context |
| 6–14 | First useful demonstration or diagnostic |
| 14–21 | A second proof, comparison, or meaningful reversal |
| 21–27 | Payoff and usable takeaway |
| 27–30 | One CTA or a satisfying ending |

A visual or sound change near a potentially confusing moment can renew orientation. It is not mandatory at a specific second.

### Approximately 45–60 seconds: case study or story

Build outcome → before → assumption → investigation → turning point → change → honest result → transferable meaning → next step. Allocate more time to the evidence and change than to introductory background.

If results have not been measured, show the process or a clearly qualitative result. Do not write invented conversion percentages to complete a case-study template.

### Narrative beats

Use status quo → disruption → attempts → reversal → consequence → meaning. At each beat, change the viewer's understanding of a goal, obstacle, knowledge, emotion, situation, time, or power. A cut alone is not a story event.

### Result-first product demonstration

Show the output → name the task → reveal a few process steps → show what the viewer can change/control → restate the useful transformation → action. Spend little time on a dashboard tour unless the screen itself proves the benefit.

## Beat table and timing

Write time, speech, visual/action, headline/progress copy, audio/edit cue, and beat purpose for every beat. Make all ranges continuous and ensure the end matches the requested duration. Mark timing provisional until the delivery is read or recorded.

Estimate speech in the relevant language, then account for pauses, demonstrations, emotional timing, and text-reading time. Do not use English word counts to judge Japanese delivery. A sparse script may still need time for a screen action; a dense line may need splitting.

Record the payoff or result shot first when practical, so the promised proof actually exists. Capture alternative hook performances, close-ups, screen inserts, and short handles around actions. Use room tone to help smooth dialogue edits.

## Edit on information change

Cut for a new idea, object, action, example, perspective, emotion, or removal of redundant material. Let the viewer finish understanding a novel point before advancing.

Ask during the rough cut: does this interval add information, evidence, emotion, orientation, or deliberate anticipation? A 1–3-second review interval can reveal dead time, but is only a heuristic. It does not require a cut, effect, or new image every 1–3 seconds.

Compress familiar setup; let novel proof breathe. Alternate compression with release. Move strong evidence earlier. Replace abstract explanation with demonstration when it improves understanding.

## Pattern changes and transitions

Use a change when it helps the viewer orient or feel a meaningful shift: talking head to screen; overview to close-up; statement to counterexample; music to brief silence; broad scene to a highlighted detail.

| Technique | Purpose | Common risk |
|---|---|---|
| Hard cut | Immediate progression | Broken continuity when context disappears |
| Jump cut | Compress time or repeated dialogue | Nervous pacing from too many tiny jumps |
| Match cut | Link actions or before/after states | Requires planned footage |
| J-cut | Let the next audio thought begin before the image changes | Competing information |
| L-cut | Keep narration over visible evidence | Covering a weak explanation with irrelevant B-roll |
| Smash cut | Emphasize contrast or a punchline | Diminished impact if repeated |
| Pan/object wipe | Move or reveal through a physical action | Gimmick without narrative reason |
| Silence or dip | Create emphasis or emotional space | Unintentional stall |

Keep effects subordinate to the idea. No transition pack is required; simple edits can work well when the progression is clear.

## Framing and readable text

- Show the main subject/action at phone size.
- Keep critical content inside a conservative central area, then preview the actual platform and placement UI.
- Use gestures, eye line, contrast, and highlights to guide attention.
- Separate large headline/progress text from accurate captions.
- Correct captions manually, especially names, numbers, terms, and multilingual compounds.
- Use short readable lines, adequate contrast, and enough display time.
- Do not put an important interface action behind captions or show several simultaneous headline messages.

Vertical 9:16 and a high-quality clean master are practical starting choices for these feeds. Check current requirements for actual delivery. Do not hard-code one safe-zone percentage for all organic posts and ad placements.

## Sound hierarchy

Voice carries the information; music carries mood/rhythm; effects reinforce events; silence provides contrast.

Keep dialogue consistent and intelligible on phone speakers. Duck the music beneath speech; avoid hard noise-floor changes between edits; retain enough breath and pause to sound human. Synchronize effects with visible actions or reveals rather than random word flashes.

Use licensed music or verified platform-cleared commercial audio for the intended account and use. Cross-platform reuse may have different rights. Avoid exact loudness specifications unless the delivery context requires and verifies them.

## Practical production passes

1. **Meaning pass:** assemble the story; remove greetings, delayed context, repeated clauses, and unsupported claims.
2. **Evidence pass:** make every important claim visible or properly qualified; bring useful proof forward.
3. **Progression pass:** clarify beat changes; remove stalls; make the payoff complete.
4. **Sensory pass:** add only framing, transitions, sound, and movement that help comprehension or emotion.
5. **Copy/accessibility pass:** proofread captions; reduce clutter; check phone-scale readability and UI overlap.
6. **Playback pass:** watch with sound, muted, and audio-only where appropriate. Confirm voice and visual explanation support the same story.

## Final handoff checklist

- First frame makes the subject, result, conflict, or useful action understandable.
- Hook relevance is clear and its promise is believable.
- Body starts fulfilling the promise without resetting into background.
- Each beat has a reason to exist.
- Payoff answers the hook; multipart content completes its current promise.
- Timing is feasible for delivery and comprehension.
- Claims, product features, testimonials, and result numbers are supported.
- Captions are accurate; headline text is readable and appropriately placed.
- Voice is intelligible; music rights match the use.
- Cover/title align with the opening and payoff.
- A distinctive brand cue fits without delaying value.
- One next step serves the objective, or the ending intentionally omits a CTA.
