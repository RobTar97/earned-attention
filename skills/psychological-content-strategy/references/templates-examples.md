## Example: 25-second product workflow

**Brief:** An app duplicates a task checklist and allows editing assignees and deadlines. Audience: team leads rebuilding repeated project tasks. Objective: qualified free trials. Available proof: a screen recording of those actions. No time-savings or conversion measurements are supplied.

**Hook options:**

1. Demonstration: “One checklist. A fresh project without retyping it.”
2. Pain: “Still rebuilding the same tasks for every project?”
3. Practical contrast: “Copy the process, then change the details.”

**Selection:** The demonstration hook matches the available screen proof and shows the transformation immediately.

| Time | Speech | Visual/proof | On-screen copy | Audio/edit | Beat purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | “One checklist. A fresh project without retyping it.” | Duplicate an existing checklist into a new project | “REUSE THE CHECKLIST” | Begin on the action; quiet click | Promise and immediate proof |
| 3–7 | “Start with the tasks you already use.” | Briefly highlight the original task list | “1. Start with your template” | Hold long enough to read | Orient the process |
| 7–13 | “Duplicate it, then update the owners.” | Show duplicate action and change two assignees | “2. Assign the new team” | Cut on the task change | Useful process evidence |
| 13–19 | “Change the deadlines for this project.” | Edit two dates | “3. Set the new dates” | Keep voice above music | Finish the demonstrated workflow |
| 19–22 | “Same process. Ready for the next team.” | Show completed checklist | “READY TO START” | Brief visual hold | Deliver payoff |
| 22–25 | “Try it free with your next project.” | Show product name and verified trial route | “Try it free” | Simple ending | One activation CTA |

Timing is provisional until read aloud and the screen actions are recorded. Ensure the free-trial terms are accurate and the CTA route exists.

**Next test:** Change only the first three seconds to the pain-recognition hook. Compare early retention and qualified trial starts, keeping the rest stable. An organic posting comparison is directional.

## Example: 30-second educational diagnosis

**Brief:** A fictional landing-page review demonstrates clearer message order. No conversion lift is measured.

**Hook:** “Before you rewrite the button, check what comes above it.”

| Time | Speech | Visual/proof | On-screen copy | Audio/edit | Beat purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | “Before you rewrite the button, check what comes above it.” | Show a CTA under a vague headline | “CHECK THE MESSAGE FIRST” | Start with screen detail | Specific promise |
| 3–8 | “Can a new visitor tell who this is for?” | Highlight a company-centered phrase | “Who is it for?” | Tight crop on phrase | Diagnostic step |
| 8–15 | “Replace the company description with the customer's task.” | Replace vague heading with a concrete task description | “Show the customer's task” | Cut to changed copy | Visible application |
| 15–22 | “Then give them one reason to believe you.” | Place an authentic product demonstration before the CTA | “Proof before the ask” | Screen insert | Credibility |
| 22–27 | “Now the button follows an explanation they can understand.” | Show the revised sequence | “Task → proof → next step” | Hold for reading | Complete payoff |
| 27–30 | “Save this order for your next review.” | Display the three-part sequence | “Save the sequence” | Soft ending | Utility CTA |

This demonstrates a clearer message sequence; it does not claim increased sales. Use user research or a controlled test to evaluate conversion effects.

## Example: retention audit with sparse evidence

**Input:** A 30-second draft opens with a logo for four seconds, promises one quick practical tip at second five, repeats background through second eighteen, gives the tip at second twenty-six, then asks viewers to like, follow, share, and buy. Supplied data only says viewers often leave before ten seconds.

**Observed:** The draft delays the subject and practical value. The data cannot identify one exact causal line.

| Priority | Evidence | Viewer problem | Change | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Logo at 0–4 s | The subject and relevance arrive late | Open on the actual tip/result; integrate branding into that scene | Early retention |
| 2 | Background occupies 6–18 s | The promised utility is delayed | Demonstrate the first useful step immediately after the hook | Retention after the hook |
| 3 | Tip at 26 s; four asks | Payoff is late and action is divided | Give full tip earlier, then one relevant CTA | Useful saves or qualified clicks |

**Rewrite:** Result visual → specific symptom → demonstrated fix → short reason → complete takeaway → saveable checklist. Supply actual wording from the user's subject; do not invent the tip.

## Example: copy-only response

**Input:** Three headlines for an app that stores reusable project checklists. Do not claim time savings.

- “Your next project starts with the checklist you already trust.”
- “Reuse the process. Update the details.”
- “Stop rebuilding the same project checklist.”

Each headline uses a supported capability. A brief note can identify the angle; no video storyboard is needed.

## Campaign matrix

| Audience moment | Primary role | Message/hook | Proof | Format | Brand cue | CTA | Primary measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recurring task frustration | Brand building | A recognizable story of starting from scratch again | Honest situation depiction | Short story | Consistent phrase/product cue | Optional next episode | Recognition and relevant reach |
| Evaluating a solution | Consideration | Show how a real task works | Actual interface demonstration | Tutorial/demo | Product appearance | See full walkthrough | Qualified visits and comprehension |
| Ready to start | Activation | Specific benefit and clear terms | Verified feature and offer | Direct-response short | Product name | Try/book/buy | Qualified conversions |

Choose a content mix from the objective and resources. This matrix is not a mandatory 60:40 posting or spending plan.

## Hook bank by purpose

Adapt the blanks to verified facts:

- Education: “The usual advice misses one condition: [condition].”
- Diagnosis: “If [symptom], check [observable detail] first.”
- Utility: “Here is [usable tool] for [specific task].”
- Product: “Watch [real capability] turn [input] into [output].”
- Comparison: “[A] changes [thing]. [B] changes [different thing].”
- Story: “I expected [outcome]. Then [real reversal] happened.”
- Prevention: “Before [action], check [proportionate risk].”
- Brand memory: “For the moment when [buying cue], [brand association].”

Avoid invented achievements, absolute superlatives, and vague “you won't believe” openings. The strongest template is the one the available proof can fulfill.
