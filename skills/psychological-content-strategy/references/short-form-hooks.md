# Short-form hooks from opening to payoff

Use this guide to develop video hook options, plan the opening beat, or audit the handoff from hook to body. For a copy-only request, select ideas here as useful and return the requested copy. For a guided start, carry forward the viewer, voice, goal, and usable material from [guided intake](guided-intake.md).

## Choose the reason to keep watching

Start with what this viewer wants to see, learn, or decide. Select a hook that the available proof can support. For a hook menu, offer meaningfully different reasons to watch and recommend one based on brand fit, viewer need, and what can be shown. For a controlled hook comparison, hold the body, promise, and CTA steady and vary the opening treatment.

Use the [hook library](hook-library.md) for adaptable starters and worked examples across product, software, and educational videos.

| Approach | Build the opening around | What the body owes |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | A specific exception to advice this audience actually hears | Show when the advice breaks down and what to do in that case |
| Prevent a loss | An observable mistake with a proportionate consequence | Show a practical check or fix. Use a deadline only when it is real and relevant |
| Curiosity gap | A question raised by an unexpected detail the viewer can recognize | Give an early clue, then answer the stated question. Use real conditions and results for a reported test |
| Result or proof first | A real artifact, visible change, or scoped result | Show how it happened and what the evidence covers. Give a baseline, period, or denominator when the number needs it |
| Visual or sound surprise | A relevant movement, detail, quiet opening, or sound that directs attention to the subject | Make the odd detail useful by explaining or using it in the next beat |
| Search intent | The viewer's actual task or question in everyday language | Deliver the answer early. Put the topic in speech, the title, or a short label where it helps the viewer |
| Audience identification | A recognizable role, task, or moment in the viewer's day | Deliver advice that changes for that audience. Name the specific constraint or need |
| Problem recognition | A concrete frustration described with care | Show what to check and give a usable next step. Keep possible causes tied to observable evidence |

Choose proof first when the result is already visible. Choose a question or an explanation when that gives the viewer a better reason to stay. A simple on-brand opening can carry the video. Use concrete objects, actions, and conditions for specificity when measured results are unavailable. For first-person experience, see [hook patterns](hook-patterns.md). For evidence from customer material, see [evidence to angles](evidence-to-angles.md).

## Plan the opening and the first useful beat together

| Channel | Decide what it does | Check it in context |
|---|---|---|
| Picture | Show a clear subject, relevant action, or piece of proof. A static close-up can be enough | View at phone size with the target placement's UI. Keep the payload, captions, and proof clear of overlap |
| Speech and sound | Start with the premise or useful action. Put intelligible speech or meaningful source sound first | Listen with the chosen music and the intended device in mind. Adjust mic placement and the mix to the actual recording |
| On-screen text | Add a short orienting label, question, or promise where useful. Keep speech captions accurate and readable | Check contrast, reading time, and competition with the visual. Place text where the composition and placement leave room |

Treat the opening seconds as an edit to preview. The three channels can do different jobs within the same promise. Check the opening with sound on and muted. The muted version should still identify the subject through the picture, accessible captions, or selective text. If a muted preview or an exact placement preview is unavailable, record the check as pending. Use the image, terms, and framing the viewer actually needs. Verify exact placement or search claims against current primary sources when the brief requires them.

Write the opening line and the next useful line or action before filling out the rest of the script. If the handoff repeats the premise, jump to the first check, proof, or useful step instead. Start inside the relevant action when feasible and keep enough context for a new viewer. Time the read aloud and leave room to see the important action.

| Handoff | Use when | Next useful beat |
|---|---|---|
| Hook → Value → Action | A direct tip, demonstration, or answer carries the asset | Deliver the first step or show early proof. Finish the answer before any CTA |
| Problem → Consequence → Solution | The consequence helps the viewer understand why the problem matters | Make the consequence observable and proportionate, then show the remedy early |
| Before → After → Bridge | The change and how it happens can be demonstrated | Show comparable states and explain the actual step that connects them |
| Obstacle → Response → Result | A story contains a real change in circumstances or decision | Connect beats by cause and consequence in natural speech. Use a new shot when it clarifies the change |

Use [short-form optimization](short-form-optimization.md) for structure selection, [story arcs](story-arcs.md) for narrative progression, and [video production](video-production.md) for timing, sound, and framing. Hold a shot long enough to read the detail. Cut or change focus when it adds information or clarifies an action.

## Close the promise

Deliver the promised answer, result, or rule before the ending. Choose a clean finish, one relevant action, or an optional visual or spoken return to the opening. An optional replay seam should preserve a complete first viewing and an intelligible cold opening. Preview end-to-start for cadence, sound, readable proof, and the time a CTA needs. Keep the clean finish if the seam obscures any of those.

For a CTA, specify the action and what the viewer receives. A keyword comment with promised follow-up belongs in the script only when the account has a verified delivery path and the promised material is ready. Use a routine action the viewer can actually take if that path is missing.

## Review the hook before filming

Use the checks that fit the deliverable. For each issue, point to a line, frame plan, or missing asset, name the viewer problem, and suggest the smallest useful fix.

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| First-frame clarity | A viewer can identify the subject or find the relevant detail without scanning competing elements |
| Listening and reading effort | Speech, captions, labels, and sound each help the viewer follow the same premise |
| Specificity and proof | The promise is concrete, supportable, and matched by the actual visual or source |
| Viewer and brand fit | The line sounds natural for the brand and names a situation the viewer recognizes |
| Search-intent fit | The wording matches the actual query and answer when discovery through search is a goal |
| Hook-to-body continuity | The next beat delivers progress toward the promised answer, and the close completes it |

For an audit, prioritize unsupported promises, unusable proof, and missing payoff before polishing the edit. If scores are requested, use the optional editorial scale in [hooks and copy](hooks-copy.md) or the user's requested scale. Mark out-of-scope dimensions as not applicable and describe scoring as editorial judgment. Offer or write variants in the scope the user asks for.

## Worked example

Fictional brief: a sewing educator with a calm, practical voice has a close-up of fabric snagging and a second clip showing how to inspect the needle on the same machine. The lesson is one diagnostic check for beginners.

| Beat | Speech | Picture | Text or sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | “Fabric snagging as you sew? Check the needle first.” | Show the snag in a close-up of the fabric | “Needle check” and readable speech captions. Keep the machine sound below the voice |
| First useful beat | “With the machine off, take a look at the needle tip. Check for a burr or a bend.” | Show the tip in the actual inspection clip | Hold the shot so the tip is readable |
| Payoff | “If you spot either, replace the needle and test on a scrap.” | Return to the fabric close-up or hold on the needle while naming the next step | End on the concrete check. For a visual return, match the fabric crop after the full takeaway |

The footage supports the diagnosis as a first check. Keep the result scoped to that lesson. Use a neutral sound mix and hold the needle detail long enough for a beginner to see it.
