# Example prompts

Replace the fictional products and constraints with your real information. Explicitly naming the skill is useful when your agent has several overlapping marketing skills.

## 1. Create a product-demo Reel

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to write a 30-second Instagram
Reel for LoopList. It saves and reuses project checklists; it does
not automate tasks. Audience: small agency team leads who repeat
similar projects. We have one screen recording and no testimonials
or measured time savings. Use warm, practical English. Give exactly
three hook angles, choose one, and deliver a timed beat table with
speech, proof, headline text, and sound/edit notes. End with one
free-trial CTA and suggest one experiment.
```

Expect a concrete opening, an honest demonstration, continuous timing, a complete payoff, and a test with a quality/business guardrail. Unseen footage is treated as a production plan, not inspected evidence.

## 2. Repair a weak educational Short

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to improve this 45-second Short.
Audience: project managers. The first four seconds show a logo.
The next eight explain why clear checklist items matter. The examples
do not appear until second 26. The ending asks viewers to like,
share, follow, and book a call. The useful lesson is to replace
"Review design" with "Check the design on mobile", "Check copy"
with "Confirm the headline names the customer outcome", and
"Test form" with "Submit it and confirm the success message".
Rank the problems using this evidence and give the complete rewrite.
Keep one CTA. Do not claim to have watched footage.
```

Expect evidence-linked priorities and an immediately useful opening. The rewritten asset should still deliver all three checks.

## 3. Interpret a retention chart

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to diagnose this 30-second video.
Retention: 3s 70%, 8s 45%, 20s 25%. A screenshot at 18s has a replay
spike. We have 10,000 starts and 40 shares; reach is unavailable.
The screenshot contains six dense lines. Separate observations from
possible causes, explain what cannot be concluded, and propose one
controlled-variable test with a primary metric and guardrail.
```

Expect hypotheses rather than a claimed causal diagnosis. A replay spike can signal value or difficulty reading; rates should use available, labeled denominators.

## 4. Write concise headlines and catchcopy

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to give three English headlines
and three Japanese catchcopy options for team leads. Product truth:
LoopList saves and reuses project checklists. We have no automation,
testimonials, or measured savings. Keep the voice calm and concrete.
Deliver only the copy and one sentence explaining the angles.
```

Expect copy-sized output. The skill should follow the requested scope instead of adding a video storyboard or full campaign plan.

## 5. Plan a small brand-and-trial campaign

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to build a six-asset organic
content plan for LoopList. We can record one screen demo and two
talking-head clips each week. Brand cues: a lime circle and
"Start from your best process". Connect useful advice to project
kickoff and wrap-up moments. Balance recognition with free-trial
acquisition, give each asset a primary measure, and explain how
we should choose the mix without assuming a fixed 60:40 ratio.
```

Expect a feasible mix of original recordings and derivatives, distinct brand-memory and acquisition measures, and a contextual allocation decision.

## 6. Adapt one concept across platforms

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to adapt this completed tutorial
for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Keep the same
demonstrated claim and complete payoff. Suggest changes to the first
frame, title/cover, caption, and CTA. Verify any exact current
platform requirement before using it. We will edit in our existing tool.
```

Expect platform-specific packaging decisions grounded in the same creative truth. Rendering or publishing requires your own authorized tool workflow.

## 7. Plan an educational video without replacing the author

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to critique my eight-minute tutorial
outline. Audience: intermediate creators who know basic editing but
struggle to finish. My real experience: I spent six weeks revising one
tutorial and finally shipped it after setting a clear acceptance check.
Outline: intro, editing tools, perfectionism story, publishing, recap.
Apply Foundation, Target, Structure, Draft, and Refine. Identify the
learning contract, research gaps to verify, and Point A → Point B change.
Give an improved causal outline and concrete feedback, not a full script.
Preserve my voice; do not invent audience reactions or results.
```

Expect specific critique, useful depth, contrast/consequence between beats, and an honest transformation. The [five-phase guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/educational-scriptwriting.md) can also draft when requested; the source's anti-AI advice does not override your request.

## 8. Turn one true story into four hook angles

For an experience-led story, use the [four-family hook guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/hook-patterns.md):

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to give four hook angles for this
real experience: I opened my tutorial with a logo and background,
then revised it to show the demonstration first. I have both cuts
but no causal retention results. Try confession, experiment,
contrarian, and lesson angles. Keep any proposed test clearly planned
and explain the proof and payoff each hook needs.
```

Expect meaningful angle differences without invented past tests, results, or personal stakes.

## 9. Replace generic hype with finished spoken copy

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to rewrite this opener and body:
"Busy agency founders, this game-changing app will transform your
productivity. Keep watching for my favorite feature."
Write as though sending it to a team lead who repeats project kickoffs.
LoopList saves and reuses checklists. It does not automate tasks, and
we have no measured time savings or testimonials. Show a real benefit
early and address the possible automation misconception where relevant.
Return only the finished spoken copy, about 20 seconds, with no CTA.
```

Expect concrete save/reuse language, a connected explanation, an honest limit, and a complete ending. The [content principles](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/content-principles.md) guide the rewrite without adding framework headings, scores, a beat table, or invented numbers. Duration remains provisional until spoken aloud.

## 10. Make a proof-led product ad

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to write a 30-second Reel for LoopList.
It saves and reuses project checklists. There is no task automation,
measured saving, or customer testimonial evidence. We have a screen demo
and an actor who can demonstrate the product but is not a customer.
Choose FAB or Picture/Promise/Proof/Push and briefly explain the choice.
Give one timed script with speech, visible proof, selective text, and
one trial CTA. Use meaningful local payoffs and preserve a natural voice.
```

Expect a supported capability-to-benefit chain and early proof. An actor is not presented as a customer with invented results. The [framework guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/short-form-optimization.md) and [story-arc guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/story-arcs.md) do not impose a shock, fixed peak interval, or delayed answer.

## 11. Audit a paid-creative report

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to inspect this fictional TikTok ad
report: 100,000 impressions, 80,000 video starts, 40,000 two-second views,
20,000 six-second views, 12,000 completions, 800 destination clicks,
40 attributed purchases, $900 spend. All counts cover the same period.
Calculate available rates with explicit denominators, distinguish the two
CVR definitions, identify unavailable metrics, and propose one next test.
We have no matched account baseline, margin, or attribution-window detail.
Does a generic benchmark prove we should scale or replace the creative?
```

Expect qualified arithmetic, missing-context notes, and a hypothesis rather than a scaling verdict. A six-second field may include early engagement, and the report does not supply three-second views. See [the metric dictionary](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/analytics-testing.md#paid-creative-metric-dictionary).

## 12. Build angles from bounded customer evidence

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to analyze these fictional notes.
R1, one review: "Setup took longer than expected."
S1, a sales question: "Will it overwrite the original file?"
D1, verified product behavior: maps CSV headers and exports a separate file.
Preserving originals and saved presets have not been verified.
These notes are permitted for internal analysis; testimonial reuse is unconfirmed.
Give a compact research brief and three different creative angles, with
source/claim/proof links, ready versus conditional status, and unknowns.
Do not invent market prevalence, savings, customer results or contact.
```

Expect separation of statements, capabilities, interpretations, and hypotheses. The [evidence-to-angle guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/evidence-to-angles.md) treats a different buying reason or proof path as an angle; synonym headlines do not supply concept diversity.

## 13. Diagnose lead quality and preserve a useful lesson

```text
Use psychological-content-strategy to review these fictional campaigns.
Control: spend 1000; 100 inquiries; 5 qualified; 2 sales.
Variant: spend 1000; 60 inquiries; 12 qualified; 3 sales. Same currency.
Allocation was not randomized; the offer changed; the variant is only 3
days old against a 14-day sales cycle. Attribution and budget are absent.
Explain descriptive costs/rates, the unresolved causes and qualification
checks, and one bounded next investigation. Draft a reusable learning
record without claiming profit, causal lift, or a universal winning hook.
```

Expect a stage-specific diagnosis, preserved maturity/confounding limits, and a qualified-demand guardrail. The [commercial-learning guide](../skills/psychological-content-strategy/references/commercial-learning.md) keeps previous account lessons contextual and treats contribution ceilings as explicit assumptions, not inferred business results.
